Theory

What is Offensive Gravity?

In basketball, gravity is the invisible force that distorts a defense. Elite shooters compel defenders to follow them everywhere on the floor — whether they have the ball or not. Their mere presence stretches the defense, opens driving lanes, creates kick-out opportunities, and shifts the entire geometry of an offense.

Standard metrics like 3-Point Percentage (3P%) are blind to this. A player who shoots 38% while a defender is in their face every possession is not equal to one who shoots 38% standing wide open. The Gravity Index and GAE exist to surface that difference.

Note: This dashboard focuses specifically on perimeter shooting gravity. Interior gravity (post-up threats, rim-runners drawing help) is a related but separate phenomenon.

The Four Pillars of the Gravity Index

Gravity is not a single number. It is a composite of four independent behavioral signals — each capturing a different dimension of how much a defense must re-organize itself around a player.

📐

1. Proximity Pressure

60% of Gravity Index

Data: Average closest defender distance (ft) on all 3-point attempts, from NBA optical tracking.

The most direct signal: how physically close does a defender get? Lower distance = tighter coverage = more gravity. We invert the z-score so that a player who draws defenders unusually close scores positively.

⚠️ The 5-foot nuance: A season average of ~5.1 ft sounds modest, but the league-wide mean sits near 6.4 ft because every player accumulates wide-open transition looks that pull their average up. Maintaining a 5.1 ft aggregate requires a defender in the 2–4 ft ("Tight") bucket on 30–40% of attempts — that is elite-tier coverage.

🛡️

2. Contest Rate

20% of Gravity Index

Data: Percentage of 3-point attempts where the closest defender was Very Tight (<2 ft) or Tight (2–4 ft).

Proximity captures the average; contest rate captures the frequency of extreme defensive commitment. A player who draws tight contest on 45% of attempts is telling the defense something very different than a player at 20%, even if their averages are similar.

This component penalizes players whose low average distance is driven by a few exceptional looks, rewarding consistent defensive pressure across the entire shooting diet.

📊

3. Volume Gravity

12% of Gravity Index

Data: Total 3-point attempts (FG3A) across the season.

Defenses don't just react to efficiency — they scheme around volume. A player who attempts 600 threes forces a team to build its entire defensive game plan around containing them. A player who takes 80 threes with similar efficiency is a nuisance, not a gravitational threat.

Volume gravity is weighted modestly (12%) because it amplifies real gravity rather than substituting for it: a high-volume, tightly guarded shooter is a different kind of weapon than a spot-up player who happens to take a lot of open looks.

💨

4. Pull-Up Rate

8% of Gravity Index

Data: Pull-up 3-point attempts as a share of total 3-point attempts.

A catch-and-shoot specialist draws gravity only when stationary. A shooter who creates off the dribble — step-backs, side-steps, pull-ups from range — must be respected everywhere on the floor at all times. That universality forces defenders to close out sooner and harder, and prohibits the defense from sagging to help on drives.

Pull-up rate is weighted smallest (8%) to avoid over-crediting volume pull-up shooters who may sacrifice efficiency, but it distinctly separates the all-court threats from pure spot-up shooters.

The Gravity Index Formula

Gravity Index (G) = 0.60 · zprox + 0.20 · zcontest + 0.12 · zvol + 0.08 · zpull
zprox — Inverted proximity z-score (lower defender distance → higher score)
zcontest — Contest rate z-score (share of Tight + Very Tight attempts)
zvol — 3PA volume z-score (total 3-point attempts this season)
zpull — Pull-up share z-score (pull-up 3PA ÷ total 3PA)

All z-scores are computed fresh within each season's player pool. G = 0 is the league average shooter. G > 0 means the defense commits more resources than average; G < 0 means the player is left relatively open.

Gravity-Adjusted Efficiency (GAE)

A player who shoots 38% while a defender is 3 feet away on every shot is objectively harder to replicate than one who shoots 38% in complete freedom. GAE captures that reality:

GAE = 3P% × [ 1 + wG · G + wT · zteam ]
wG — Gravity weight (user-controlled, 0–25%)
G — Composite Gravity Index (four-pillar score above)
wT — Team Impact weight (user-controlled, 0–5%)
zteam — On-court Net Rating z-score (team quality context)

The user-adjustable weights preserve the metric's raw 3P% as a baseline (weights at 0 → GAE = 3P%) and let analysts explore how much value they believe defensive gravity and team quality should contribute. The default settings (G: 5%, Team: 1.5%) produce modest, conservative adjustments.
