Theory explained

What is "Offensive Gravity"?

In basketball, "gravity" refers to a player's ability to draw defenders towards them, even when they don't have the ball. Elite shooters act like magnets; defenses refuse to leave them open. This creates spacing for their teammates, making the entire offense more efficient.* However, standard statistics like 3-Point Percentage (3P%) do not capture this hidden value.

* Note: While "gravity" in basketball also applies to dominant interior players (e.g., rim-runners or post-up threats drawing help defense inside), this dashboard specifically focuses on quantifying perimeter / 3-point shooting gravity.

How We Calculate the Gravity Index

To quantify gravity, we use NBA tracking data that measures the Closest Defender Distance for every shot. We specifically analyze all 3-point attempts (both pull-ups and catch-and-shoot).

If a player is constantly shooting with a defender right in their face (a low average defender distance), it means defenses are terrified to leave them open. We calculate the difference between the league average defender distance (about 6.5 feet) and a player's average defender distance, and then convert this into a Z-score to create their Gravity Index.

  • Gravity Index > 0: Above average gravity. Defenders stay glued to this player. A score of +1.5 means they draw significantly more defensive attention than average.
  • Gravity Index < 0: Below average gravity. Defenders sag off and help inside. A negative score (e.g., -1.5) means they are routinely left wide open compared to the rest of the league.

Gravity-Adjusted Efficiency (GAE)

We believe a player who shoots 38% while heavily guarded is mathematically more valuable to an offense than a player who shoots 38% but is left wide open by the defense.

To account for this, we multiply a player's Standard 3P% by their Gravity Index. The result is Gravity-Adjusted Efficiency (GAE). This metric rewards players who maintain high efficiency despite defensive pressure, revealing the true floor-spacers of the NBA.

Understanding the Plots

1. GAE vs Standard 3P%

This scatter plot compares the traditional metric (X-axis) with our new metric (Y-axis). Players sitting above the diagonal dashed line are actually more valuable than their raw shooting percentage suggests, due to their gravity. Players below the line are the opposite; they might have better percentages than others, but aren't necessarily better shooters.

2. Proximity vs Standard Efficiency

This chart plots Defender Distance (X-axis) against Standard 3P% (Y-axis). The size of the bubble represents the player's Total 3-Point Volume. The ideal player is in the top-left quadrant: heavily guarded, yet highly efficient on high volume.

3. Gravity Impact Dumbbell Plot

This plot dramatically shows the "shift" for specific players. The red dot is their standard 3P%, and the green dot is their GAE. A long line pulling to the right indicates a massive boost in value due to their extreme offensive gravity.
